# ECHO-128 — The Standing Doors

> [!s3] Classification
> **Designation:** E.C.H.O.
> **EC:** LOC — Location
> **ESC:** S3 — Fractured
> **RCC:** RCC-2 — Systemic Failure
> **RTS:** T4 — Grand
> **RDS:** C — Divergent

---

## Description

ECHO-072 presents as an interior corridor of indeterminate length, lined on both sides with identical wooden doors set at even, regular intervals. Field measurements taken during the initial survey recorded no fewer than four hundred doors along a single visible stretch, though the corridor's true extent has never been established; investigators who continue walking past the two-hour mark report no change in the door pattern, spacing, or lighting conditions ahead of or behind them.

The corridor is understood to be a persistent architectural fragment of a T4-classified reality catalogued under Investigation Site R-052, first entered during the Initiative's seventh operational cycle. No exterior structure, foundation, or supporting building has ever been located; the corridor does not appear to connect to any recoverable floor plan, and Echo Research Division has been unable to determine whether it represents a single structure, a repeated segment, or a composite of several source locations fused during collapse.

---

## Observed Behavior

Observed Behavior

Each door along ECHO-072 is mechanically sound and opens without resistance. Approximately one in every thirty doors opens onto a shallow, fully furnished room consistent with mid-20th-century domestic interiors from the source reality — a bedroom, a kitchen, a study — each undisturbed and each entirely enclosed, with no windows, no additional doors, and no signs of prior habitation beyond furniture and fixtures. The remaining doors open onto flat, matte-grey surfaces positioned directly behind the doorframe, close enough to touch, which Device Development Bureau instrumentation has been unable to characterize as either solid or absent.

Rooms do not remain fixed to the doors that produced them. A door documented as opening onto a kitchen during one pass has, on subsequent passes by the same team, opened onto a different room, a grey surface, or occasionally nothing distinguishable at all. No pattern governing this reassignment has been identified despite multiple controlled surveys using marked doors and fixed reference points.

Psychological Effects

Personnel report a persistent, low-grade sense of having already visited whichever room they are currently standing in, regardless of whether the room's contents are unfamiliar. This effect intensifies with time spent inside ECHO-072 and has been the primary limiting factor on investigation duration, independent of any physical hazard. Two investigators from separate survey teams have independently described an inability to recall, once outside the corridor, how many doors they had opened — despite complete equipment logs confirming the count.

---

## Manifestation Pattern

ECHO-072 does not manifest intermittently; the corridor has remained continuously accessible at its documented entry point since first contact, with no observed variation in structure across seven years of monitored access. Entry and exit are possible only through the single known threshold; no secondary access point has been located, and attempts to breach the corridor's walls or ceiling have not been authorized pending further Archive Operations review.

I no longer open doors past the two-hour mark, not because of any incident, but because I have stopped trusting that I would notice one.
